## 1. Product Overview

PayOps simulates the core workflow of a payment processing system.

A merchant enters a fictional card number and payment amount. The Payment Switch validates the request and forwards it to a separate Fake Bank service. The bank simulates an approval or decline based on the card's simulated balance.

Approved transactions are stored in PostgreSQL and recorded in a double-entry ledger. Settlement reconciliation compares payment records against a bank-provided CSV file and identifies matching, mismatched, and missing transactions.

If the bank does not respond within the configured timeout, the payment is marked `PENDING`. A background worker subsequently checks the bank's transaction status and finalizes the payment.

Idempotency prevents duplicate processing when the same request is retried.

**This is an educational payment simulator. It must not process real card payments or connect to real banking networks.**

### Expected payment flow

1. Merchant submits a fictional card number and amount.
2. Payment Switch validates the request, including the Luhn checksum.
3. The switch forwards the request to the Fake Bank service.
4. The bank checks the simulated card balance and returns an approval or decline, or delays its response.
5. The switch records the payment status and relevant references.
6. Approved payments generate balanced debit and credit ledger entries exactly once.
7. Timed-out requests become `PENDING` and are resolved by background processing.
8. Authorized users can view payment records according to their roles.
9. An administrator uploads a settlement CSV, reviews reconciliation results, and exports the results.

- Handle approvals, declines, timeouts, and failures.

### C. Fake Bank service

- Maintain fictional cards and simulated balances in the database.
- Approve or decline transactions according to the configured simulated balance and rules.
- Prevent duplicate debits when a request is retried.
- Provide a way to query the status of a bank transaction.
- Simulate a delayed response for a card ending in `9`, with approximately a 30-second delay.
- Ensure delayed requests can eventually be resolved without creating a second debit.

Use these fictional test cards where appropriate:

### D. Payment statuses and timeout recovery

- Support `APPROVED`, `DECLINED`, and `PENDING`.
- The switch should wait approximately five seconds for a synchronous bank response.
- If the bank does not respond in time, return or record `PENDING` without treating the transaction as declined.
- Use a background worker to query the bank and finalize the transaction when its result becomes available.
- Ensure recovery is safe to retry and does not duplicate a debit or ledger posting.
- Do not block a request for the entire simulated 30-second delay.

### E. Double-entry ledger

- Record approved payments using debit and credit postings.
- Ensure the total debits equal the total credits for each applicable transaction.
- Create ledger entries exactly once, even if payment processing or recovery is retried.
- Do not create approved-payment ledger entries for declined transactions.
- Preserve the ledger's integrity through database transactions and appropriate uniqueness constraints.

### F. Settlement reconciliation

- Allow an authorized administrator to upload a bank settlement CSV.
- Parse CSV data safely, including quoted fields and escaped quotes.
- Match bank records against application payment records using appropriate references and available transaction fields.
- Identify matched records, mismatches, duplicates, and transactions missing from the bank file.
- Support the existing reconciliation result types, including `MISSING_IN_BANK`.
- Export reconciliation results as CSV with correct escaping.
- Provide understandable validation and error messages for invalid files.
- Do not add an elaborate reporting or analytics dashboard.

### G. Authentication and authorization

- Use Spring Security and JWT.
- Support `ADMIN` and `MERCHANT` roles.
- An administrator can view all authorized payment records and perform reconciliation administration.
- A merchant can access only their own payment records.
- Validate JWT signatures and authorization claims correctly.
- Do not rely on frontend restrictions for access control.
- Do not introduce hardcoded production secrets or insecure fallback secrets.

### H. Database and persistence

- Use PostgreSQL through the existing Supabase project.
- Persist payments, payment events where applicable, merchant and user records, ledger entries, reconciliation results, and Fake Bank data.
- Use database constraints and transactions to protect data integrity.
- Preserve payment idempotency and prevent duplicate bank debits and ledger entries.
- Use explicit, version-controlled SQL migrations rather than relying on automatic schema creation in production.
